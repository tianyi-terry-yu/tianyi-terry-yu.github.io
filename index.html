SAMPLE PATENT WRITING

Application US/19,009,819 STATUS: Response Filed Aug 2026

Final Office Action Response (Video Coding)

Technology: Cross-Component Prediction Coding

Drafted in response to a Final Office Action rejecting claims under §102/103 over two references (Luo, Wang) directed to a video-coding claim reciting an adjustment to the relationship between luma and chroma samples. Full case details can be accessed at USPTO Global Dossier. Excerpt from the traversal:

Claims 1-19: Distinguishing the Cited Art

"It is seen that Luo at Para. [0032] merely describes a hierarchical organization of video data ... and that these segments 'may be sampled at different resolutions.' ... For a POSITA, such disclosure merely teaches to either upsampling the segment 308 as a whole ... or downsampling the segment 308 as a whole .... In the context of such resolution adjustment of the frame segment, the corresponding relationship between luma and chroma samples within the frame segment remains unchanged. ... Therefore, Luo at Para. [0032] fails to disclose or suggest claimed feature (1).

Moreover ... It is seen that, Luo at Fig. 4 discloses at most 'a block diagram of an encoder 400 according to implementations of this disclosure.' Notably, stage 402 is labeled as 'INTRA/INTER PREDICTION,' a generic prediction stage that Luo does not describe as performing any cross-component prediction operation, let alone adjusting a corresponding relationship between luma and chroma samples. ... Furthermore, as acknowledged in the Office Action, Wang merely discloses a video processing system wherein the prediction coding tool is a cross-component prediction coding tool. Wang is also completely silent about adjusting a corresponding relationship between luma and chroma samples."
A — distinguishes a resolution-scaling operation (upsampling/downsampling) from the claimed luma/chroma adjustment — same paragraph, different operation entirely.
B — a figure's label isn't its disclosed function. "INTRA/INTER PREDICTION" names a category, not the specific operation the rejection required.
C — closes the combination: even paired with Wang, neither reference actually discloses the claimed adjustment.

Claim 20: Product-by-Process Strategy

Claim 20 was separately rejected under §102(a)(2) as anticipated by Luo. Rather than traverse it, I canceled Claim 20 and added new Claim 21, recasting the same technical content as a straight method claim:

21. A method for storing a bitstream of a video, comprising:

  obtaining a first video block, the first video block being generated based on a first adjustment of a current video block of the video, the first adjustment comprising at least one of adjusting an orientation of the current video block or adjusting positions of samples in the current video block;

  generating a second video block based on a second adjustment of the first video block, the second adjustment being an inverse process of the first adjustment;

  generating the bitstream based on the second video block; and

  storing the bitstream in a non-transitory computer-readable recording medium,
  wherein a corresponding relationship between luma and chroma samples of the first video block is adjusted for a cross-component prediction coding tool.
A — MPEP § 2113: product-by-process claims are patentable or not based on the product itself, not the process used to make it. Novel process steps don't rescue a claim if the resulting product is structurally the same as the prior art's.
B — recasting the same steps as a method claim shifts examination onto the steps themselves rather than the resulting product, sidestepping the §2113 problem instead of arguing around it.
C — the luma/chroma limitation carries over unchanged, preserving the same substantive scope while changing claim type.
